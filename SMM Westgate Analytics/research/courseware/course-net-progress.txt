Progress Reports: Raw Data


For any company or individual running an inbound marketing campaign, a consistent audit of these important metrics is vital to managerial decision making.  Whether is about identifying your:

Brand Awareness: the total number of users, new users, sessions, or pageview on your website
Brand Acquisition: whether consumers are coming to your site due to email, social channels, organic search, or backlink referrals from other websites,
Brand Retention: tracking through goals how many users are completing your call-to-actions, subscribing to your email campaign, and opening/clicking-through your actual emails.

In all of these instances, managerial decision-making should be based on the analytics.  In future chapters, we will address the specific strategies related to these numbers, but for now, it is important to realize that a company or individual must identify these metrics BEFORE the campaign in order to measure the impact of the content decisions.  Therefore, for the sake of the practicum, we have developed a progress report system that will be used during the entire campaign through our Social Media Magnet plugin.  This website analytics reporting mechanism should be a guide to each user about how the campaign is performing and how to adjust it appropriately.  Once the practicum has begun, to access these weekly analytics, you will need to go to The Social Media Magnet dashboard (not your WordPress dashboard) and click on the blue hyperlink called “Website Analytics.”  Here you will find the following data to help you complete your three campaign progress reports.  Directions on these reports will be included in your Level 4 Assignments.  But for now, let’s examine these reports.

First, you will be provided the raw data for your website and MailChimp email campaigns on a weekly basis.  As you will see below, data will be collected across four items for each of the categories of brand awareness, brand acquisition, and brand retention per week, that is, from Sunday to Saturday.  The plugin will run a cron job (that is an automatic data pull) for each student website every Sunday to report your weekly metrics.  Note: Metrics will be reported cumulatively each week, meaning that Week 3’s report will include all of the data for Week 1, 2, and 3.  This cumulative reporting allows you to see how your website is growing each week.  Like an annuity investment, if you are performing the campaign correctly, then you should see compounded growth each week, as the previous week’s blogs will still be helping increase traffic in future weeks (that is, if it is good content). The raw data will look like this:

For awareness, the plugin will track how many users, new users, sessions, and pageviews you accumulated on your website each cumulative week.  The awareness measure is a function of how well your content is being perceived and how much traffic is flowing back to your website.  Good content is the impetus here.  If you have content with slugs that interest people, then they will click on your social links to come back to read the full blog article.  For acquisition, these numbers tell you where you are being most successful in driving your traffic back to the site.  Specifically, these acquisition channels refer to:

Direct: Most of these website users are related to your email campaign.  Users are clicking on your email links and are pushed “directly” back to your website from the email link.  (Note: People who come in via the category of “Email” are typically users who have come in on a forwarded email from someone else.  Even though Google Analytics will demonstrate this category separately from Direct, we do NOT measure the “Email” category because it is not a user due to your primary action, again, since it was a forwarded email from someone else.)
Social: These are users who are clicking on links within social media platforms, most notably Facebook, Instagram, Twitter, LinkedIn, etc.  A deeper look into just the social channel will tell you which social channels are most productive in driving traffic to your site.
Organic Search: Users in this category are finding your website through search engines like Google, Bing, and Yahoo.  Search engine optimization and selection of the right keywords will help increase traffic in this category.
Referrals: Whenever your content is shared by another website and that users ethically create a backlink URL to your site, then these users who click on that backlink and end up at your site are called Referrals.

Next, the retention category measures the completion of the goals that have been set up on your website.  Specifically, you are required to set up a “Follow-Me” goal that will track the number of users who subscribe to your email campaign.  This “destination” goal tracks the number of users from your MailChimp sign-up form to your sign-up thank you page.  Building goal funnels in Google Analytics is a very important step in tracking the percentage of users who are completing your call-to-actions and how many abandon the goal funnel process.  If your abandonment rate is too high, then there is often a problem with the goal funnel structure.  Lowering this rate will increase your goal completion numbers and increase your grade.  Also within the retention category, the plugin will monitor your email campaign progress at MailChimp, recording your overall subscribers, email opens, and email click-throughs.  This is very important because the OVERALL goal of this entire organic process is to move users from the top of the goal funnel through to the bottom, which in this class is signing up email subscribers and retaining them as part of the Social Media Magnet theory.  Remember, your project overview looks like this:

Progress Reports: Grades

Additionally, within the Website Metrics, students will also see below the raw data a set of grades associated with each of the single items (e.g. users, new users, sessions, etc.).  These grades are calculated from the mean and standard deviation for each of the single items across each student in your class. (Note: If you do not understand how mean and standard deviation are used to calculate a grade, then please ask your professor for an example.  We have a FAQ that will explain it to you.)  Like we have said before, this class is based on competition.  Just like the real world, you are competing against every other blogger in your domain, every other company in your product or service category, every other employee at your work for the promotion, and realistically, you are even competing against yourself to grow your social impact week over week, month over month, and year over year.  We have found that many students have trouble with the competition side of this practicum, but we implore you to get used to this aspect.  As soon as you graduate, competition becomes the name of the game in a free-market society.  And just like the real world, your practicum grade in this course is based on how well you do on each of these metrics versus the other students in your class.  These per item grades will appear like this:

Along with each of the per item grades, you will also see a categorical grade, one each for Brand Awareness, Brand Acquisition, and Brand Retention.  These categorical grades are just averages for each of the four items in the category.  However, the revelation with these categorical grades is to realize how well (or poorly) you are doing in any one category.  The category that has the lowest grade is one that you should probably focus on more diligently in order to raise your overall grade.  For example, in the demonstration below, this student is doing pretty well with his or her email campaign because the grade in the retention category is stronger at 87.06%.  But the student is worst at driving traffic to his or her website because the awareness category grade is only 72.39%.  Therefore, the student should focus on creating stronger, more relevant content.  This poor content could be due to just overall weak content on his or her website blog, or it could be due to not blogging or posting enough in his or her social media channels.  A content audit with another student in his or her group could lead to the revelation of why his or her content is failing, and could potentially assist with the acquisition grade as well.  Better content should lead to more users to be acquired from his or her social media platforms, which obviously would help the acquisition grade as well.

Note: Remember, ALL (each item grade and categorical grade) are cumulative grades, starting Week 1 of the campaign through the current week’s grades.  Therefore, the weekly grade, highlighted in purple, is a cumulative overall grade for the practicum.  In reality, this IS your current overall practicum grade.  In other words, if the practicum were to end this week, the weekly grade would be your final grade for the course practicum.  Be sure to check your professor’s syllabus to see how much (what percentage) of the final grade in this course is determined by the final grade for the course practicum.

Your practicum is starting soon.  Good luck, and please remember, your practicum grade is HIGHLY CORRELATED with how hard your work on your campaign.  If you put in the time, then in most instances, you will receive the reward of a grade that is standard deviations above the mean.  But be sure to start at a sprint because like the annuity metaphor, each week’s metrics are compounding.  You want to get the full compounding effect over the entire campaign, so start strong and keep running the race.  It’s a marathon, but you can do it, and who knows, you might even enjoy it.
