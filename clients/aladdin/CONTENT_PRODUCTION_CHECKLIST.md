# Aladdin Content Production Checklist

**Approved:** October 27, 2026  
**Applies to:** Aladdin blog articles, campaign emails, Facebook posts, and Instagram posts.

Use this as the release checklist for each weekly campaign. Record any exception before scheduling or publishing; do not mark an unchecked item complete.

## 1. Ground the campaign

- Read the current approved Strategy Canvas, Message Matrix, Brand Tone of Voice, 45-day calendar, and *The Social Media Magnet Pro Publishing Edition Book* before drafting. Review the latest relevant dated analytics and assessments as supporting evidence.
- Check published and scheduled content before planning the week. Avoid repeating a recent subject, caption, or image.
- Keep Aladdin's positioning consistent: one trusted local team for the surfaces, rugs, air, and restoration moments that matter most. Use careful evaluation, clear explanations, local accountability, and realistic expectations. Do not introduce an unapproved named offer.
- Map the weekly subject, audience question, useful answer, next action, destination, and measurement for each piece.

## 2. Make blog, email, and social work together

- Build one connected weekly sequence. The blog develops the main subject; the email and social posts adapt its ideas for their channels without copying the same text.
- Make each email section support a relevant part of the week's campaign. Link each section to its matching live article or service page. Confirm the article is live before the email or a post points to it.
- Give each piece one clear next action and a working path to the relevant service and an existing inquiry form or phone number. Use the appropriate carpet, upholstery, rug-care, or other service page. Keep Rug Gallery shopping separate from rug-cleaning inquiries.
- Verify every destination and the full route to inquiry. Use post-specific tagged links where approved, and confirm the link resolves correctly.
- Check campaign timing: the blog, email, and paired social posts must appear in an order that lets readers follow the links when they encounter them.

## 3. Follow the approved email format

- Use the established Aladdin template: logo, **three distinct content sections**, a relevant text link within each section, and the established red footer. Do not add a body CTA button.
- Keep the three sections useful and distinct while supporting the week's blog and social themes. The email should advance the same customer journey, not function as an unrelated newsletter.
- Set and review section headings, body copy, and section links at **18 pt**, unless the user approves a different size. **Always specify and check type in points. Never measure type in pixels.**
- Verify the sender, subject, preview text, links, phone number, footer, and scheduled date. Inspect saved desktop and mobile previews after the final edit.

## 4. Pair and adapt every social post

- For every planned post concept on a date, create a Facebook and Instagram companion on that date. Both cover the same specific subject and use the same image; write platform-specific copy.
- Facebook: include one relevant, tested article or service link and its approved post-specific tracking. If the link is placed in the first comment, the caption must clearly direct readers there.
- Instagram: include one clear call to action with the approved current number, **210-736-1821**, until a different social number is confirmed. Confirm the number before scheduling.
- Make captions concrete and helpful. Answer the customer's question, explain the process or decision, and give a meaningful next action. Do not rely on generic care-plan language, meta-commentary about a photo, or disclaimers as the substance of a post.
- Confirm every intended calendar date has the intended platform pair and that both posts are actually scheduled.

## 5. Match each actual image to its actual copy

- Inspect the **rendered image and caption in each scheduled Facebook and Instagram preview**. For each one, verify that the visible action, setting, people, and service support the caption's specific claim or advice. A filename, image brief, or draft pairing is insufficient.
- Reuse an image for its Facebook/Instagram companion pair. Use a different image for every distinct Facebook post; do not recycle the same image across different post concepts in the week.
- Prefer natural, photo-driven scenes with people where relevant. Reject implausible actions, awkward poses, icon cards, and visuals that feel like the same post repeated.
- If a worker is shown, the approved clothing is a **burgundy/maroon polo and khaki/tan work pants**. Never use blue or green work shirts.
- Label generated scenes as illustrations in production notes. Never present them as photographs of an actual Aladdin appointment, employee, customer, or result. Use authentic Aladdin-owned images for actual-work claims. Do not invent before-and-after results, testimonials, customer details, or employee identities.
- If image and copy do not match, replace the image or rewrite the copy, then inspect both platform previews again.

## 6. Release gate

Review every item in the scheduling and email interfaces after saving, not just in the production draft:

- [ ] Foundational sources and current calendar consulted; repetition checked.
- [ ] Blog, email, and social form a connected sequence with live, relevant inquiry paths.
- [ ] Email uses the approved three-section template and 18 pt type; desktop and mobile previews checked.
- [ ] Each planned social concept has its Facebook and Instagram companion on the intended date.
- [ ] Every Facebook and Instagram image visibly matches its own caption.
- [ ] Distinct Facebook concepts use distinct images; clothing and authenticity rules checked.
- [ ] Facebook links, Instagram phone number, and all destinations verified.
- [ ] Every caption has one useful message and one clear next action.
- [ ] Actual scheduled/published status and timing verified for every item.
- [ ] Unresolved checks or exceptions listed explicitly; certification withheld until resolved.

**Final sign-off record:** campaign week; reviewer; review date; post-by-post preview checks; link and phone checks; email preview checks; exceptions and fixes; final status.
